Shared Pointer Lab — Short Answers

ENGLISH

Answer each question once, in either language, in the answer area after both versions.
These answers are reviewed manually, separately from the automatic score of 100.

Q1 [S1–S3] How does RAII release resources during exception unwinding? Why must
all handles in an ownership group share one counter instead of independent counts?

Q2 [S2] Compare get(), bool, and use_count() for shared_ptr<int>{},
shared_ptr<int>{nullptr}, and shared_ptr<int>{static_cast<int*>(nullptr)}.
Why is checking only the stored pointer insufficient when releasing a control block?

Q3 [S4–S5] How do copy, move, self-copy, self-move, and move assignment between
distinct handles in one group affect counts? Does std::move itself move anything?

Q4 [S4–S6] Why can releasing old ownership before reading other break p = p->next
or p = std::move(p->next)? Describe a safe ordering of operations.

Q5 [S2, S6–S7] Who cleans up if object allocation succeeds but block allocation
fails? What guarantee must reset(new T(...)) provide? How do the lab's two
allocations differ from the combined allocation commonly used by std::make_shared?

Q6 [S1] What do const shared_ptr<T> and shared_ptr<const T> each restrict?
Why does operator*() const return int& when T is int?

Q7 [S8] Which nodes survive after releasing one of two lists with a shared tail?
Draw the ownership relationships. Why does use_count not count reachable paths?

Q8 [Discussion; no implementation required] Why cannot reference counting reclaim
cycles? How does weak_ptr help? Why is this lab not thread-safe? What limitation
can recursive destruction of a very long linked list encounter?

shared_ptr<int>{static_cast<int*>(nullptr)} 的 get()、bool 和 use_count()。

Q4 [S4–S6] 为什么先释放旧所有权再读取 other，可能使 p = p->next 或

什么限制？
